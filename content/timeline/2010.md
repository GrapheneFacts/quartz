**Evidence:** Well-supported

## The citation

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awards the Nobel Prize in Physics to Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov “for groundbreaking experiments regarding the two-dimensional material graphene.”

That wording matters. The prize is for **experiments**, not for inventing the word, not for Wallace’s Hamiltonian, not for Boehm’s oxide flakes.

## What the prize changed

- Funding and national roadmaps accelerate. The EU path toward the [[timeline/2013|Graphene Flagship]] is already in motion.
- Graphene becomes shorthand for a wider **2D materials** family (hBN, TMDCs, later twisted stacks).
- Industry communications get louder than the device data. The hangover — quality definitions, graphene-washed additives, unmet silicon-replacement claims — is still the texture of [[timeline/2014-2019]] through [[timeline/2026]].

## Monday-post caution

Nobel year is a good hook and a bad place to stack unverified product claims. Pair 2010 with one *current* well-supported item (an order, a paper, a regulator note) if you need a today-bridge. Do not treat the prize as evidence that a 2026 press release is true.
