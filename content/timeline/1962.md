**Evidence:** Well-supported (with later historiographic argument about what “first graphene” means)

## What Boehm’s group reported

Hanns-Peter Boehm and colleagues work from **graphite oxide**, reduce it, and report extremely thin carbon films, including flakes identified as **monolayer**. The tools are the TEM / diffraction lineage of mid-century surface and colloid carbon chemistry — not a Hall bar on SiO₂.

Geim and Novoselov have publicly pointed at this line as an experimental precursor. The usual careful wording is:

- **1962:** monolayer carbon from the oxide route, seen and argued as one atom thick.
- **2004:** isolation on an insulating substrate plus *electronic measurement* that made the 2D electron gas / Dirac picture inescapable and launched the citation boom.

Both sentences can be true. Fighting over the word “first” is mostly branding.

## The name

**Graphene** as a systematic name for a single graphite sheet is associated with Boehm and co-authors in the mid-1980s (often cited **1986**: graphite + *-ene*). See [[timeline/1963-2003]].

## Why Monday posts go wrong here

- “Nobody saw monolayer carbon before 2004” — false.
- “Boehm built the iPhone of materials” — also false. No devices, no global field, no Dirac-fermion transport paper.
- Oxide-route carbon is chemically dirtier than a cleaved crystal. That distinction still matters for batteries, inks, and anything called “graphene” on an invoice.
