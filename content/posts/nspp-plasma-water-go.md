## Five facts

1. Banavath et al. grow graphene oxide at a methane–water interface with a non-thermal atmospheric nanosecond pulsed plasma (NSPP). No mined graphite, no Hummers acids.
2. Journal: *Nature Communications*, DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-69831-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69831-0). Received 9 Aug 2025, accepted 9 Feb 2026. Texas A&M plus LTEOIL (College Station).
3. Reported lab rate: a four-gap reactor up to about 5 g GO per day. That is a bench scale-up, not a plant.
4. Hydrogen is the co-product. The project started as a hydrogen effort; carbon sheets were the unexpected high-value solid.
5. Trade coverage on 1 Oct 2026 (Chemical Engineering) restates the July 2026 university release. It is not a new experiment.

## What the process is

Conventional GO starts with graphite, oxidizes it (Brodie, Staudenmaier, Hummers and variants), then exfoliates. That route needs concentrated acids and leaves a waste stream.

NSPP does the opposite. Methane is the carbon source. Water is the substrate. A nanosecond pulsed discharge at ambient pressure breaks methane into reactive carbon fragments and splits water into oxygen-containing species. Those meet at the liquid surface and deposit an oxygen-functionalized carbon network. Because the “substrate” is a replenishable water surface, product does not have to be scraped off a reactor wall to keep the run going.

Authors named on the paper: Ramu Banavath, Yufan Zhang, Mirza Akhter, Shegufta T. Upama, Sayyam Deshpande, John D. Lassalle, Matthew Payne, Abu Shoaib Saleh, Howard B. Jemison, Rollie Stanich, Micah J. Green, Kunpeng Wang, David Staack. Corresponding: Green, Wang, Staack.

Public characterization claims (AFM and related checks, as summarized by the university and Graphene-Info, 4 Jul 2026): single-layer GO, oxygen content and flake size described as controllable, properties said to be comparable to commercial GO. “Comparable” is a materials claim in the paper’s framing, not an application qualification.

## Limits

- 5 g/day is not bulk supply. Collection, washing, drying, batch-to-batch C/O ratio, metal impurities, and sheet-size distribution still have to be shown at larger gap counts.
- Energy per gram and a full life-cycle comparison with Hummers GO are not settled by the press quotes.
- “Bulk production of a sheetlike nanomaterial from something other than graphite is unprecedented” (Green, Chemical Engineering, 1 Oct 2026) is a strong process claim. Other methane-to-carbon routes (plasma blacks, CVD graphene on metal) exist; the specific claim is continuous GO at a plasma–water interface.
- Industry partner LTEOIL sponsored the work. That is disclosed, not disqualifying. Cost language from the sponsor is not a filing.
