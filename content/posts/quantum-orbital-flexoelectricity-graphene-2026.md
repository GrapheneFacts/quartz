## What it is

Flexoelectricity is polarization from a strain gradient. In graphene nanowrinkles whose apex radius approaches the carbon–carbon bond length, the effect becomes quantum orbital flexoelectricity: extreme curvature asymmetrically perturbs the out-of-plane π orbitals, producing local polarization densities of order 1–4 C m⁻²—five to seven orders of magnitude above typical mesoscale flexoelectric systems.

Iyengar, McHugh, Salvage, Vajtai, Gadhamshetty, Dalton, Tripathi, Ajayan, Meunier et al., “Sub-Nanometer Curvature Unlocks Quantum Orbital Flexoelectricity in Graphene,” *Advanced Materials* 2026 (DOI 10.1002/adma.202518224). Popular coverage 27–28 October 2026.

## Measurements

Atomic-force microscopy, Raman strain mapping, Kelvin-probe force microscopy (work-function shifts), and conductive AFM. Threshold voltage for wrinkle-associated current ~1 V, matching the calculated band offset ~1.2 V. Response strength tracks apex sharpness far more than wrinkle height.

## Limits

Naturally formed wrinkles so far; deterministic large-area patterning and device integration remain open. Local effect, not yet a macroscopic product.
