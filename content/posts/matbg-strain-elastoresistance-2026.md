University of Washington researchers (Ma, Yankowitz, Chu and colleagues) reported in *Science* (12 November 2026) that controlled strain applied to magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene produces electrical resistance changes more than 100 times larger than those seen in ordinary metals. The gauge factor exceeds conventional metals by more than two orders of magnitude; the effect is further amplified at low temperature.

MATBG is two graphene monolayers rotated by approximately 1.1°, forming a moiré superlattice that flattens the Dirac bands and hosts correlated electronic states. Strain continuously tunes the local lattice and therefore the effective twist and band structure. Because the physics is hypersensitive to precise geometry, small strains move the system across phase boundaries and produce a large electrical readout.

The result supplies an additional experimental knob that does not require changes in doping or magnetic field. The authors plan to simplify the piezoelectric apparatus so other groups can reproduce the measurement and apply it to other atomically thin materials.

Evidence label: Well-supported (peer-reviewed *Science* paper, DOI 10.1126/science.adz0597).

Limits: largest effects remain at low temperature; absolute response still depends on the precise local twist of each device; not a room-temperature sensor claim.
