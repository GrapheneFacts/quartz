## Why this page exists

Labs, vendors, and slides use one word for monolayer film, few-layer powder, GO paste, and a 20-layer platelet. Purchasing and toxicology fall apart when those are mixed. ISO/IEC nanotechnology vocabulary is the boring attempt to stop that.

## Families to open first

- **ISO/TS 80004** nanotechnology vocabulary series — graphene-related terms live in the 2D-material / carbon nanostructure parts as they are issued and revised.
- **ISO/TS 21356** family — structural characterization of graphene, typically Raman, microscopy, thickness language for flakes and films.
- **IEC** graphene electronics vocabulary where a device paper cites it.

Exact part numbers move when ISO revises. Cite the year on the cover page you held.

## How this site uses the words anyway

| Site word | We mean | Do not hear |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Graphene | Honeycomb carbon sheet; say monolayer if we mean one layer | Any black powder |
| FLG | A few layers, counted when the paper counts them | GO paper |
| GNP / nanoplatelet | Thicker stack sold as graphene | 2004 Manchester flake |
| GO | Oxidized sheets from graphite oxide | Licensed vaccine antigen |
| rGO | GO with oxygen pulled back | Defect-free CVD |
| CVD film | Grown then often transferred | 4,000 t powder |

## What a standard is not

Not a purity certificate for a bag. Not a health clearance. Not proof a company is ISO-certified because they printed the letters.
